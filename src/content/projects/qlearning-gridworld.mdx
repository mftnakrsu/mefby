## Overview

Three assignments from a course on planning and decision making: a custom grid world, a Q-learning agent that solves it, and a deep Q-network for a continuous maze. The grid world has an Ice Age theme, in which Scrat crosses a 7×12 grid of ice obstacles, danger zones and small rewards to reach one of two goals.

## How it works

The environment follows the Gymnasium API, with three actions (up, down and right) and an observation of the row, the column and one flag: whether the agent has already visited the lover cell, which multiplies the goal reward by six. Because the flag is part of the state, the Q-table has four dimensions and the agent can learn one policy before the visit and another after it. Exploration is epsilon-greedy, with a reverse-sigmoid decay that stays exploratory early and settles late.

## Result

After 500 training episodes the agent reaches a goal every time, by the best route: through the lover cell to the far goal, for a reward of 686 in 17 steps, where a random agent succeeds about 5% of the time. The DQN for the continuous maze, with experience replay and a target network, reached the goal in 100 episodes in a row. Heatmaps of the Q-values and arrow plots of the policy show what was learned.
