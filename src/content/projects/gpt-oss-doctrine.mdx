## Overview

A LoRA fine-tune of OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss-20b, a mixture-of-experts model with about 21 billion parameters of which about 3.6 billion are active, that answers questions grounded in 58 publicly released, unclassified doctrine documents. Training and serving ran entirely on the Tinker API, so no local GPU was needed at any point.

## How it was built

GPT-5.4 generated 18,730 question–answer pairs from the documents, split by chunk so that test questions never share a source passage with the training data. A rank-16 adapter trained for two epochs. A GPT-5.4 judge then compared it with the base model on 1,873 held-out pairs for accuracy, completeness and grounding, and the adapter won 82.6% of them (95% CI 80.9–84.4%). Judging 300 pairs again with randomized positions moved the result by less than two points. A Hugging Face Space puts both models side by side.

## What it taught me

The largest change was behavior, not knowledge. The reasoning-tuned base model spent its whole budget on hidden reasoning and usually gave no final answer; the adapter answers directly, in about 49 tokens instead of 297. Where the base model did answer, the adapter still won 77.9% of the time. Validation loss during training sat on a different scale from loss on the inference path, and neither tracked the judged quality, so the judge became the measure that counted.
